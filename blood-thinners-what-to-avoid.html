Blood pressure medications explained

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

There are several families of blood pressure medicines, and many people take more than one. Understanding the basics helps you take them well and know what to watch for.

The main types

  • ACE inhibitors (names ending -pril, like lisinopril) — relax vessels; a dry cough is a known side effect.
  • ARBs (ending -sartan, like losartan) — similar to ACE inhibitors, usually without the cough.
  • Calcium channel blockers (ending -dipine, like amlodipine) — relax vessels; may cause ankle swelling.
  • Diuretics ("water pills") — remove extra fluid and salt; best taken in the morning.
  • Beta blockers (ending -olol, like metoprolol) — slow the heart; don't stop suddenly.

What to watch for

  • Dizziness when standing (especially when starting or combining).
  • ACE/ARB plus potassium supplements or salt substitutes can raise potassium.
  • NSAIDs (ibuprofen) can blunt blood pressure medicines and stress the kidneys.
Keep taking them: High blood pressure is usually silent, so don't stop when readings look good — that's the medicine working. Track home readings and share them at visits.
