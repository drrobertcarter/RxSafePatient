Can I crush my pills?

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

If swallowing pills is hard, crushing them seems like an easy fix — but for some medicines it's unsafe. Here's how to tell the difference.

Pills you usually should NOT crush

  • Extended-release / long-acting (often marked ER, XR, SR, CR, LA, XL) — crushing releases the whole dose at once, which can be dangerous.
  • Enteric-coated — the coating protects your stomach or the medicine; crushing defeats it.
  • Certain hormone, chemo, or hazardous medicines — the dust can be harmful to handle.
  • Capsules with tiny beads — usually shouldn't be crushed, though some can be opened onto food (ask first).

Safer options for trouble swallowing

  • Ask if the medicine comes as a liquid, patch, dissolvable tablet, or smaller pill.
  • Ask whether an immediate-release version can be split or crushed instead.
  • Try swallowing techniques (a full sip first, head position) your pharmacist can show you.
Always ask first: Your pharmacist can tell you in seconds whether a specific pill is safe to crush, split, or open — and often has a better alternative.

The takeaway

Never assume a pill is safe to crush. Check with your pharmacist, especially for anything labeled extended-release or enteric-coated.
