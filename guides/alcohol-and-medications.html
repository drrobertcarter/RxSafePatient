Alcohol and medications: what to watch for

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

Alcohol is a drug too, and it can interact with a surprising number of medicines — sometimes making them stronger, sometimes adding harmful effects. Here's a plain-language overview.

Common risky combinations

  • Sedatives, sleep aids, opioids, and some anxiety medicines — alcohol adds to drowsiness and can dangerously slow breathing.
  • Acetaminophen (Tylenol) — regular drinking plus acetaminophen raises the risk of liver damage.
  • NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) — alcohol adds stomach-bleeding risk.
  • Certain antibiotics (metronidazole, tinidazole) — can cause flushing, nausea, and vomiting.
  • Diabetes medicines — alcohol can cause low blood sugar.
  • Blood pressure medicines — alcohol can add to dizziness and low pressure.
Check the label: Many medicine labels carry an "avoid alcohol" warning. If you're unsure, ask your pharmacist how much (if any) is okay with your specific medicines.

The takeaway

You don't always have to avoid alcohol entirely, but some combinations genuinely aren't safe. Check your medicines and ask before mixing — especially with anything that makes you sleepy.
