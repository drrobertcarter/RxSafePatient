Do antibiotics affect birth control?

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

This is one of the most common medication questions — and the answer is more specific than the popular belief. Here's what the evidence actually says.

The common myth

Many people believe all antibiotics make birth control pills fail. For most common antibiotics (like amoxicillin), current evidence does not show a meaningful reduction in birth control effectiveness.

The real exception

The antibiotic that clearly matters is rifampin (and related "rifamycin" drugs, used for tuberculosis and some other infections). Rifampin speeds up how the body clears hormones and can make hormonal birth control less effective.

What to do

  • If you're prescribed rifampin/rifabutin, ask about backup contraception.
  • Being sick with vomiting or diarrhea can reduce absorption of the pill — use backup if that happens.
  • When unsure, use a backup method and ask your pharmacist.
Bottom line: Most antibiotics don't require backup contraception, but rifampin does. Your pharmacist can confirm for your specific antibiotic.
