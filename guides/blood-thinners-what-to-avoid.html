Blood thinners: what to watch for

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

Blood thinners prevent dangerous clots, but they also mean bleeding is easier. Knowing what affects them helps you stay in the safe zone.

Medicines that add bleeding risk

  • NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) and aspirin.
  • Other blood thinners or antiplatelets taken together.
  • Some supplements: fish oil, vitamin E, ginkgo.
  • Certain antibiotics and antifungals can raise warfarin's effect.

Food and warfarin (vitamin K)

If you take warfarin, vitamin K (in leafy greens) works against it. You don't have to avoid greens — just keep your intake consistent day to day, and tell your clinic before starting a vitamin-K supplement. Newer blood thinners (apixaban, rivaroxaban) aren't affected by vitamin K the same way.

Everyday care

  • Use a soft toothbrush and be careful shaving.
  • Tell dentists and surgeons you take a blood thinner before any procedure.
  • Wear a medical ID.
Warning signs: black or tarry stools, vomiting blood, pink/red urine, heavy bruising, or bleeding that won't stop. A serious fall or head injury on a blood thinner needs urgent evaluation.
