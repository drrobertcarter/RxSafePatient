Generic vs. brand-name drugs: are they the same?

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

Generic medicines can cost a fraction of the brand-name price, which leads many people to wonder if they're really equivalent. Here's the plain-language answer.

What "generic" means

A generic contains the same active ingredient, in the same strength and dose form, as the brand. In the U.S., the FDA requires generics to be bioequivalent — meaning they work in the body essentially the same way — before approval.

What can differ

  • Inactive ingredients — fillers, dyes, or coatings can differ, which occasionally matters for allergies or sensitivities.
  • Pill appearance — color, shape, and markings vary, which can be confusing but doesn't change the medicine.

When to pay extra attention

For a few narrow-margin medicines (like certain thyroid, seizure, or blood-thinner drugs), some people and doctors prefer to stay consistent with one manufacturer. If you switch and notice a change, tell your pharmacist.

Money-saving tip: Ask your pharmacist, "Is there a generic for this?" — and whether a discount card or program lowers the cost further.

The takeaway

For most people, FDA-approved generics are a safe, cost-saving choice. If you have concerns — especially with a narrow-margin medicine — talk to your pharmacist.
