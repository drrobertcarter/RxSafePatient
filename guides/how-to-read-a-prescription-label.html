How to read a prescription label

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

A prescription label holds everything you need to take a medicine correctly — if you know what to look for. Here's a quick tour.

The key parts

  • Your name — always check it's you.
  • Medicine name and strength — e.g., "Lisinopril 10 mg."
  • Directions (the "sig") — how much, how often, and when (e.g., "Take 1 tablet by mouth daily").
  • Quantity and refills — how many, and how many refills remain.
  • Prescriber and pharmacy — who wrote it and where to call.
  • Fill and expiration/discard dates.

The warning stickers

Those colored stickers matter: "take with food," "may cause drowsiness," "avoid alcohol," "finish all medication," or "avoid sunlight." Don't ignore them.

Common abbreviations

  • PO = by mouth · QD/daily = once a day · BID = twice a day · TID = three times · PRN = as needed · HS = at bedtime.
If anything's unclear — the directions, a warning, or an abbreviation — ask your pharmacist. Misreading a label is a common, avoidable cause of medication mistakes.
