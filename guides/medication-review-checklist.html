Medication review checklist

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

A medication review is when a pharmacist or doctor looks at everything you take to check for interactions, duplicates, and medicines you may no longer need. It's one of the highest-value things you can do for your safety. Here's how to prepare.

Gather before you go

  • Every prescription medicine (bring the bottles or a printed list).
  • All over-the-counter medicines — pain relievers, allergy, sleep, antacids.
  • Vitamins, supplements, and herbal products.
  • Any creams, patches, inhalers, eye drops, or injections.
  • Your allergies and past medicine reactions.
  • Your main health conditions.

Questions to ask during the review

  1. Do any of these interact with each other?
  2. Am I taking anything twice (duplicate ingredients)?
  3. Is there anything I may no longer need?
  4. Are the doses still right for me?
  5. Should any be taken at different times to avoid interactions?
  6. Do any need routine lab monitoring?
Hidden-ingredient tip: Combination products (like some cold or pain medicines) can contain acetaminophen or other ingredients you're already taking. A review helps catch accidental doubling.

After the review

  • Update your master medicine list with any changes.
  • Note any new timing instructions.
  • Ask for a printed summary to keep.
