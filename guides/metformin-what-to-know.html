Metformin: what to know

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

Metformin is often the first medicine prescribed for type 2 diabetes. It's well-studied and effective — here's how to take it well.

How to take it

  • Usually taken with food to reduce stomach upset.
  • Extended-release versions can ease side effects — ask if it's right for you.
  • Take it consistently; don't double up on missed doses.

Common side effects

Stomach upset, nausea, and diarrhea are common early on and often fade. Starting low and going slow helps. Long-term use can lower vitamin B12 — your doctor may check it.

When extra care is needed

  • Kidney function matters — metformin is cleared by the kidneys, so it's adjusted or paused if kidneys are impaired.
  • Around imaging with contrast dye or major surgery, it may be paused temporarily.
  • Serious illness with dehydration warrants a call to your provider.
Alcohol: Heavy drinking with metformin can raise a rare risk — ask your pharmacist what's safe for you.
