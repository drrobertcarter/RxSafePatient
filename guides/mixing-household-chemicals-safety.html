Which household chemicals should never be mixed?

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

Medication safety isn't the only kind of "what's safe together." Some everyday cleaning products become dangerous when combined. Here are the big ones to never mix.

Never mix these

  • Bleach + ammonia → releases toxic chloramine gas.
  • Bleach + acids (vinegar, some toilet-bowl or rust removers) → releases toxic chlorine gas.
  • Bleach + rubbing alcohol → can form chloroform and other harmful compounds.
  • Different drain cleaners together → violent, dangerous reactions.

Also be careful: many "all-purpose" and glass cleaners contain ammonia, and many descalers contain acids — so mixing cleaners without reading labels can accidentally combine these.

Warning signs of exposure

Coughing, burning eyes/nose/throat, chest tightness, or trouble breathing after using cleaners. Get to fresh air right away.

If it happens: Move everyone to fresh air, open windows, and call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 (U.S.). For trouble breathing, chest pain, or collapse, call your local emergency number.

Safer habits

  • Use one product at a time; rinse surfaces between products.
  • Never mix cleaners in the same container.
  • Store chemicals separately, in original labeled containers, away from children.
  • Ventilate while cleaning.
