NSAIDs and your kidneys

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

Ibuprofen and naproxen are handy for pain, but they can be hard on the kidneys in certain situations. Here's when to be careful.

How NSAIDs affect kidneys

NSAIDs can reduce blood flow to the kidneys. For healthy kidneys and short-term use, that's usually fine — but risk rises with dehydration, illness, older age, and certain combinations.

The "triple whammy"

A well-known higher-risk combination is an NSAID + an ACE inhibitor or ARB (blood pressure) + a diuretic (water pill). Together, especially during dehydration, they can reduce kidney function.

Who should be extra careful

  • People with chronic kidney disease.
  • People on the blood-pressure combinations above.
  • Older adults and anyone dehydrated (vomiting, diarrhea, hot weather).
Sick-day tip: If you're vomiting, have diarrhea, or can't keep fluids down, ask your pharmacist whether to pause NSAIDs (and sometimes certain blood-pressure medicines) until you recover.

Safer options

Acetaminophen is often gentler on the kidneys for pain — but the right choice depends on you. Ask your pharmacist, especially if you have kidney concerns.
