Polypharmacy: taking many medicines at once

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

"Polypharmacy" simply means taking several medicines — often five or more. It's common and sometimes necessary, but the more medicines you take, the higher the chance of interactions, side effects, and confusion.

Why it raises risk

  • More medicines means more possible interactions.
  • Effects can add up — several sedating medicines, or several that affect the kidneys.
  • Duplicate ingredients can hide in combination products.
  • Complex schedules make missed or doubled doses more likely.

Special concern in older adults

Aging changes how the body handles medicines, and some drugs (like strong anticholinergics or long-acting sedatives) carry extra risk of confusion and falls in seniors.

What helps

  • A medication review with a pharmacist to spot interactions, duplicates, and medicines you may no longer need (deprescribing).
  • One up-to-date master list, shared with every prescriber.
  • One pharmacy when possible.
Ask the key question: "Is there anything on this list I no longer need?" Fewer medicines, when appropriate, often means fewer risks.
