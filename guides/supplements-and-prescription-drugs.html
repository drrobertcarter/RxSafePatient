Do supplements interact with prescription drugs?

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

"Natural" doesn't mean "no interactions." Vitamins, minerals, and herbal supplements can change how prescription medicines work — sometimes significantly. Here's what to know.

Common supplements that can interact

  • St. John's Wort — can lower the levels of many medicines (some antidepressants, birth control, blood thinners, transplant drugs) and raise serotonin risk.
  • Vitamin K (and leafy greens) — can counteract warfarin; consistency matters.
  • Calcium, iron, magnesium, zinc — can block absorption of some antibiotics and thyroid medicine if taken together.
  • Fish oil, vitamin E, ginkgo — may add to bleeding risk with blood thinners.
  • Potassium — can add up with certain blood pressure medicines.
Tell your pharmacist everything: Include every vitamin and herbal product on your medicine list. Supplements are a common blind spot because people don't think of them as "drugs."

The takeaway

Before adding a supplement, check it against your medicines and ask your pharmacist. RxSafeCheck lets you add supplements and food factors alongside prescriptions to see possible interactions.
