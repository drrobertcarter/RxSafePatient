What to do if you miss a dose

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

Almost everyone forgets a dose sometimes. What to do depends on the medicine and how long it's been — so the safest first step is often to check the label or ask your pharmacist. Here's general guidance.

General rule of thumb

  • If you remember soon after the missed time, you can often take it then.
  • If it's almost time for the next dose, it's usually best to skip the missed one and continue as normal.
  • Do not double up to "catch up" unless your pharmacist or the label says it's okay — doubling can cause side effects or harm.

When timing matters most

Some medicines are more sensitive to missed or late doses, including:

  • Parkinson's medicines (like levodopa), where timing affects how you move.
  • Blood thinners and heart-rhythm medicines.
  • Insulin and diabetes medicines.
  • Certain HIV, transplant, and seizure medicines that need steady levels.
  • Antibiotics, where finishing the full course matters.
Best move: Keep your medicine's information sheet, and when in doubt, call your pharmacy — they can give you an answer specific to your medicine in minutes.

Prevent missed doses

  • Use a pill organizer or blister pack.
  • Set phone alarms or reminders for each dose.
  • Tie doses to daily routines (meals, brushing teeth).
  • Refill before you run out.
