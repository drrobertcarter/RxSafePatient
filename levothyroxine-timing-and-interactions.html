How to store medications safely at home

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

Where and how you store medicines affects how well they work and how safe your home is. A few simple habits make a big difference.

Store most medicines cool, dry, and dark

  • Avoid the bathroom cabinet — heat and humidity from showers can break down medicines.
  • A bedroom drawer or kitchen cabinet away from the stove is usually better.
  • Keep medicines in their original labeled containers.
  • Check labels for ones that need the refrigerator (some insulins, liquids, eye drops).

Keep everyone safe

  • Store medicines up, away, and out of sight of children and pets.
  • Use child-resistant caps, and consider a locked box for opioids or other high-risk medicines.
  • Don't share prescription medicines with anyone else.

Dispose of old medicines properly

  • Use a drug take-back site or pharmacy drop box when possible.
  • Check the FDA "flush list" for the few medicines safe to flush.
  • Otherwise, mix with something unappealing (like used coffee grounds) in a sealed bag before trashing, and scratch out personal info on the label.
Twice a year, do a medicine check: toss expired items, and review your list with a pharmacist to catch duplicates and interactions.
