Medications that cause drowsiness

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

Drowsiness is one of the most common — and most underestimated — medication effects. Alone it's manageable; combined, it can be dangerous, especially behind the wheel.

Common culprits

  • Sedatives and sleep aids (benzodiazepines, zolpidem).
  • Opioid pain medicines.
  • Some antihistamines (diphenhydramine/Benadryl, and "PM" products).
  • Muscle relaxants.
  • Some antidepressants, antipsychotics, and anti-seizure medicines.
  • Alcohol adds to all of the above.

Why combinations are risky

Each of these depresses the nervous system. Stacked together, they multiply sedation — raising the risk of falls (especially in older adults), impaired driving, and, with opioids plus sedatives, dangerously slowed breathing.

Safety steps: Ask your pharmacist about timing doses to reduce daytime drowsiness, avoid alcohol, and don't drive until you know how a combination affects you.
