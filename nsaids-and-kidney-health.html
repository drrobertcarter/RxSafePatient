NSAIDs and blood thinners: a combination to watch

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

Over-the-counter pain relievers feel harmless, but if you take a blood thinner, common anti-inflammatories deserve extra care. This is one of the most frequent — and preventable — interaction risks.

What are we talking about?

NSAIDs (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) include ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin), naproxen (Aleve), diclofenac, and others. Blood thinners and antiplatelets include warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban, clopidogrel, and even daily aspirin.

Why the combination raises bleeding risk

  • NSAIDs affect platelets (which help clotting) and can irritate the stomach lining.
  • Blood thinners already reduce clotting.
  • Together, the effects add up — raising the chance of stomach or internal bleeding.

Warning signs to know

Contact your prescriber or seek care for black or tarry stools, vomiting blood, unusual or heavy bruising, pink or red urine, or bleeding that won't stop. Sudden severe headache or weakness needs emergency care.

Ask, don't guess: Acetaminophen (Tylenol) is often suggested instead of NSAIDs for people on blood thinners — but the right choice depends on you. Your pharmacist can recommend a safer option and dose.

The takeaway

If you take a blood thinner or daily aspirin, check before reaching for ibuprofen or naproxen — including in combination cold and pain products. RxSafeCheck flags this interaction and groups bleeding-risk medicines together so nothing is missed.
