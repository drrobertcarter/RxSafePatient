Opioid safety at home

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

Opioid pain medicines can be an important part of treatment, and they carry real risks. A few safety steps protect you and everyone in your home.

Store them securely

  • Keep opioids in a locked box or cabinet, out of sight of children, visitors, and anyone at risk.
  • Never share opioids with anyone else.
  • Dispose of unused opioids promptly at a take-back site or pharmacy drop box.

The most dangerous combination

Opioids plus benzodiazepines (like alprazolam, lorazepam, diazepam), other sedatives, or alcohol can dangerously slow breathing. This combination carries a boxed safety warning. Tell every prescriber about all of these.

Know about naloxone

Naloxone is a rescue medicine that can reverse an opioid overdose. Many pharmacies provide it without a prescription. If opioids are in your home, ask whether you should keep naloxone on hand and learn how to use it.

Signs of an overdose

  • Very slow or stopped breathing.
  • Can't be woken; limp body.
  • Blue or gray lips or fingertips; pinpoint pupils.
Emergency: For a suspected overdose, call your local emergency number right away and give naloxone if available. In the U.S., Poison Control is 1-800-222-1222.
