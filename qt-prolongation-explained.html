Prescription safety guide for caregivers

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

Managing someone else's medicines is a real responsibility. This guide pulls together the essentials so you can do it confidently and safely.

1. Keep one master list

List every medicine, dose, time, and reason — including OTC medicines and supplements. Share it with every doctor and pharmacist, and bring it to every visit.

2. Set up a reliable system

  • Weekly pill organizer or pharmacy blister packs.
  • Reminders/alarms for each dose.
  • One pharmacy so interactions and duplicates get caught.
  • Refill calendar with a buffer.

3. Know the interaction red flags

Watch for new confusion, drowsiness, dizziness or falls, easy bruising or bleeding, and rashes. Be cautious with medicines that add up drowsiness or affect the kidneys. In older adults, "anticholinergic" medicines can affect memory and balance.

4. Prepare for appointments

  • Bring the master list and any side effects noted.
  • Ask: "Is anything here no longer needed?" and "Do any of these interact?"
  • Request a printed summary of changes.

5. Plan for emergencies

  • Keep the medicine list somewhere findable (fridge, wallet card, phone).
  • Know Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222 (U.S.).
  • Know which symptoms mean "call emergency services now."
