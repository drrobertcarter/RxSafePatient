Questions to ask your pharmacist before a new prescription

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

Your pharmacist is one of the most accessible experts on your medicines — and you don't need an appointment. Before you start any new prescription, a few good questions can help you take it safely and avoid surprises. Here's a simple checklist you can use at the counter or by phone.

The core questions

  1. What is this medicine for, and how will I know it's working?
  2. How and when do I take it? With food or without? Morning or night? Any missed-dose instructions?
  3. What side effects are common, and which ones should I call about?
  4. Does this interact with my other medicines, supplements, or foods? Bring your full list — including over-the-counter items and vitamins.
  5. Should I avoid alcohol, grapefruit, or driving?
  6. How long will I take it, and what happens if I stop?
  7. Is there a lower-cost or generic option?

What to bring with you

  • A current list of everything you take — prescriptions, OTC medicines, vitamins, and supplements.
  • Any allergies or past reactions to medicines.
  • Your main health conditions (for example, kidney or liver problems, pregnancy).
Tip: Keep one up-to-date medicine list and show it every time. Many interactions are missed simply because one prescriber didn't know about a medicine from another. You can build and print that list with the free RxSafeCheck tool.

Why this matters

Medicines that are perfectly safe alone can sometimes interact when combined — affecting the heart's rhythm, bleeding risk, kidney function, or how sleepy you feel. Asking these questions up front is one of the simplest ways to lower that risk.
