Serotonin syndrome: warning signs and triggers

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

Serotonin syndrome is an uncommon but serious reaction that can happen when medicines that raise serotonin are combined. Knowing the signs — and the combinations that can trigger it — helps you stay safe.

What can trigger it

It usually involves two or more serotonin-raising medicines, such as:

  • Antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, some others).
  • Certain migraine medicines (triptans).
  • Some pain medicines (tramadol, meperidine) and cough medicine (dextromethorphan).
  • Certain supplements (St. John's Wort) and the antibiotic linezolid.

The risk is highest when starting or increasing one of these, or combining an MAOI with another serotonin medicine.

Warning signs

  • Agitation, restlessness, or confusion.
  • Fast heartbeat, high blood pressure, sweating.
  • Shivering, muscle twitching or stiffness.
  • Diarrhea, high fever (in severe cases).
When it's an emergency: High fever with muscle stiffness, severe agitation, or confusion after a medication change needs urgent care — call your local emergency number. Milder new symptoms after combining serotonin medicines are worth a prompt call to your prescriber.

Lowering your risk

Tell every prescriber and pharmacist about all your medicines, including OTC cough medicine and supplements. RxSafeCheck flags serotonin-raising combinations and the specific MAOI pairings clinicians avoid.
