Statins and muscle pain: what to watch for

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

Statins (like atorvastatin, simvastatin, rosuvastatin) lower cholesterol and prevent heart attacks and strokes. Some people get muscle aches — usually mild, but occasionally a sign of something that needs attention, especially with certain drug combinations.

Common vs. concerning

  • Common: mild, symmetrical muscle aches or tiredness, often manageable with a dose or statin change.
  • Concerning: severe muscle pain or weakness, or dark, cola-colored urine — a rare but serious reaction (rhabdomyolysis) that needs prompt care.

Interactions that raise muscle risk

Certain medicines raise statin levels and, with them, muscle risk — including some antibiotics (clarithromycin, erythromycin), antifungals (itraconazole, ketoconazole), and others. Some statins (simvastatin, lovastatin) are more sensitive to this. Grapefruit can also raise levels of some statins.

Don't just stop: Statins protect your heart, so don't quit on your own. Tell your prescriber about muscle symptoms — often a lower dose or a different statin solves it. And check new medicines against your statin.

What to do

Report new muscle pain or weakness, especially with dark urine or fever. Check any new antibiotic, antifungal, or other prescription against your statin — RxSafeCheck flags these muscle-risk combinations.
