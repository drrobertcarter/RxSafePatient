Should I take my medicine with or without food?

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

"Take with food" or "on an empty stomach" isn't a suggestion — it can affect how well a medicine works and how it feels. Here's why.

Why some medicines say "with food"

  • Food can reduce stomach upset (common with NSAIDs, steroids, metformin).
  • Food can help absorption of certain medicines.

Why some say "empty stomach"

  • Food can block absorption of some medicines (like levothyroxine and some antibiotics).
  • These often work best taken well before or after eating.

Food-timing that specifically matters

  • Levothyroxine (thyroid): usually taken on an empty stomach, separate from coffee, calcium, and iron.
  • Levodopa (Parkinson's): high-protein meals can blunt it — ask about timing.
  • Some antibiotics: dairy, calcium, and iron can reduce absorption.
Keep it consistent: When a medicine is sensitive to food, taking it the same way each day matters as much as the instruction itself. Your pharmacist can help you build a simple schedule.
