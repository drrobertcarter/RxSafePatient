Traveling with medications: a safety checklist

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

A little planning keeps your medicines safe and accessible on the road. Here's a practical checklist for trips near and far.

Before you go

  • Pack enough for the whole trip plus a few extra days.
  • Keep medicines in their original labeled containers.
  • Bring a copy of your medicine list and prescriptions.
  • For controlled medicines or injectables, carry a note from your prescriber.

Flying

  • Keep medicines in your carry-on, not checked luggage.
  • Medically necessary liquids are generally allowed through security — declare them.

Time zones

For most medicines, shifting a dose by a few hours is fine. For time-sensitive ones (like insulin, some heart or seizure medicines, or contraceptives), ask your pharmacist how to adjust across time zones before you leave.

Keeping medicines cool

For refrigerated medicines (like some insulins), use an insulated travel case with a cold pack, and avoid freezing. Never leave medicines in a hot car.

International trips: Some medicines legal at home are restricted abroad. Check the destination's rules and carry documentation.
