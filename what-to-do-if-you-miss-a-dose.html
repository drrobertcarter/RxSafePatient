What is deprescribing?

By the RxSafeCheck team · Educational, reviewed for plain-language clarity · Morning Mind LLC

More medicine isn't always better. Deprescribing is the planned, supervised process of reducing or stopping medicines that may no longer help — or may be causing more harm than good.

Why it's done

  • To reduce interactions and side effects.
  • To stop medicines whose original reason has passed.
  • To simplify complex routines (fewer missed or doubled doses).
  • To match treatment to your current goals and health.

It's a careful process — not "just stopping"

Some medicines must be tapered slowly (certain heart, seizure, antidepressant, and steroid medicines). Deprescribing is done with your prescriber and pharmacist, with a plan and monitoring — never abruptly on your own.

How to start the conversation

  • Ask: "Is there anything on my list I may no longer need?"
  • Bring your full, current medicine list.
  • Mention side effects, costs, or medicines you find hard to take.
Especially valuable for older adults taking many medicines, where reducing the load can lower fall and confusion risk. A pharmacist-led medication review is a great place to start.
